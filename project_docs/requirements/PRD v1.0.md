## 1\. Introduction

### Context

**Satview Broadband Ltd** has spent a few years developing a **UV foot-sanitizing floor unit** for hospitals. A person steps onto the device, and specially engineered UV LED strips (effective range dissipating within ~1 inch of the surface to prevent eye exposure) kill pathogens on the soles of their footwear, reducing room-to-room cross-contamination in high-traffic clinical areas such as nurse stations, operating rooms, and surgical suites.

The current working prototype runs on an Arduino board and relay: 120V AC wall power is stepped down to 24V for eight UV LED strips (75 mA each) and 5V for the Arduino, with a push button triggering a 30-second UV cycle via relay. The objective of **this iteration** is to replace the Arduino-based, hand-wired prototype with a **professional custom PCB**.

The PCB to design will be a low-voltage (24V DC) board carrying the microcontroller, relay, power regulation, configurable-timer DIP switches, and plug-in connectors for all external components, designed for reliability and ready to scale to mass manufacturing. All 120V AC remains in an external certified power supply and never touches the board, keeping the PCB outside medical/safety certification scope. Mechanical/enclosure design, PSU design, regulatory certification, and UV strip sourcing are out of scope for this engagement.
